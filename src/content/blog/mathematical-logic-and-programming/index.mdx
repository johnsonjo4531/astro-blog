Some may ask why get a background or study math when you just want to become a programmer?
Well I'll give a few examples of when math comes in clutch especially the type of math reasoning
that comes from a course like MIT's Mathematics for Computer Science (which is freely viewable online
at MIT OpenCourseWare) which is sometimes taught at other universities such as mine as Discrete Math.
I didn't truly love and indulge in Math until I took a Discrete Math class at my local University.
The fancy thing about Discrete Math too is that Calculus seldom comes into play so most things are learnable with a strong grasp on K-12 math like algebra.

Positive Reasons to learn something like Discrete Math as a programmer:
- Adds to your Reasoning capabilities.
- From a set of first principle assumptions (typically called axioms) allows you to prove things that you otherwise would have to constantly question yourself about.

For example with logic we can know things like DeMorgan's Law that $\neg (A \wedge B) \iff (\neg A \vee \neg B)$ (i.e. in plain english not(A and B) is equivalent to (not A or not B)  or something like !(A && B) == (!A || !B) in JS, C++, etc.). 

Why would something like that be useful though you might ask? Sometimes the answer is clarity and other times the answer is that small changes in logic can at times have an impact with performance, and when the performance is big enough of a deal sometimes it makes sense to optimize.
